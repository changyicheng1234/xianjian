# 先见系列 · Episode 001: Would Republicans lose both chambers in the 2026 midterms?

[← All episodes](README.en.md) · [中文](issue-001.md)

**Original estimates: GOP loses House 71.2%, Senate 65.6%, both 59.0%.**

> Retrospectively added on November 11, 2026 from the October 31 frozen archive. The election had already happened by this publication. This is not evidence that the forecast was publicly available on GitHub on October 31.

The video's “lame-duck president” wording is a colloquial title; the actual questions concern congressional organizational control.

## Original reasons

Sabato's October 27 House ratings placed 225 seats at least leaning Democratic, 193 Republican and 17 toss-ups. Ratings are judgments, not seats already won.

The Senate analysis counted all 100 seats, not only the 35 elections. Its organizational baseline was 47 Democratic-allied and 53 Republican seats; holding existing seats and gaining four would reach 51. Competitive-state polling offered paths, not guaranteed simultaneous wins.

One Kansas survey of 800 likely voters showed a 3.5-point initial Democratic lead and 2.4 points after leaners. These are variants of one survey, not independent evidence or a national seat estimate.

## Strongest countercase

A lost Democratic-held Senate seat could leave only 50 seats even after four gains. Holding either chamber would defeat the joint event.

The Post–Schar Kansas survey disclosed completion rates among contacted registered Democrats 32% versus Republicans 12%. This flags that survey's nonresponse risk, not candidate support or equal bias in other surveys. Applying a state Senate-survey warning to the House is itself a limitation.

## Method and limitations

AI assessed evidence direction and strength, while the engine discounted duplication, credibility and relevance. Weights were heuristic and subjective, not calibrated on a large resolved-event population or validated seat simulation. The three questions were researched separately with shared material; the joint 59.0% was not a product of the marginal probabilities. Algebraic consistency does not validate dependence assumptions.

## What happened afterward?

AP's November 5 account states it called Democratic Senate control at 23:36 ET on November 3 and House control at 01:24 ET on November 4. The forecast lean agrees with those calls.

AP's election calls do not settle the original questions. Resolution waits for the new Congress first to complete both chambers' organization, using formal Speaker/caucus/organizational records. Unresolved or shared control must not be forced into a binary result. The term begins January 3, 2027; organization need not finish that day. One directionally aligned event does not establish calibration or superior probability accuracy.
